Getting a corrupted microSD card error on your Nintendo Switch Lite?
Don't panic—in this video, I'm gonna show you 3 proven solutions to fix it without losing any of your saved data.
Let's jump right in!

SOLUTION 1: REPAIR THE MICROSD CARD USING WINDOWS
* First up, turn off your Nintendo Switch completely.
* Open up the card slot cover and take out the microSD card.
* Insert the microSD card into your computer using an SD card reader.
* Press the windows key on your keyboard.
* Type " **cmd** " in the search bar.
* Right-click on **" Command Prompt "** from the search results over here.
* And select **" Run as administrator "** .
* Click **" Yes "** if the User Account Control prompt appears.
* In the command prompt window, type in: **chkdsk E: /f /r**
* Make sure to replace " E: " with the actual drive letter assigned to your microSD card in File Explorer.
* Hit enter on your keyboard.
* Wait for Windows to scan the drive and repair any corrupted sectors.
* Once the scan finishes, safely eject the microSD card from your computer.
* Slide the microSD card back into your Nintendo Switch and power it on.
* Finally, check if your games and data load without any error messages.

If Windows fails to repair the card or the drive is still unstable, let's move on to the next solution.

SOLUTION 2: MANUALLY BACK UP AND REFORMAT THE SD CARD
* First up, turn off your Nintendo Switch and take out the microSD card.
* Connect the microSD card to your computer using your card reader.
* Open up **" File Explorer "** on your PC.
* Click on your microSD card drive from the left sidebar over here.
* Right-click on the folder named **" Nintendo "** .
* And select **" Copy "** .
* Go over to your computer's **" Desktop "** .
* Right-click on an empty space and select **" Paste "** to create a safe backup.
* Once the copy is finished, safely eject the microSD card from your PC.
* Slide the microSD card back into your Nintendo Switch and turn the console on.
* From your home screen, open up **" System Settings "** from the bottom toolbar.
* Scroll all the way down on the left sidebar and select **" System "** .
* Look over here on the right side and scroll down to select **" Formatting Options "** .
* Let's tap on it.
* Select **" Format microSD Card "** from the options over here.
* Select **" Continue "** and confirm to format the card.
* Wait a few moments for your Nintendo Switch to format the card and restart automatically.
* Turn off your console again and plug the microSD card back into your computer.
* Copy your backed-up **" Nintendo "** folder from your desktop.
* And paste it directly onto the root of your freshly formatted microSD card.
* Once the transfer is complete, safely eject the card from your PC.
* Insert the microSD card back into your Nintendo Switch and turn it on.
* Finally, check if your games and data load properly without any issues.

If only a specific game is still giving you a corruption error, proceed to the final solution.

SOLUTION 3: CHECK AND REDOWNLOAD CORRUPTED GAME DATA
* First up, open **" System Settings "** from your home screen.
* Scroll down over here on the left sidebar and select **" Data Management "** .
* Once you're inside **" Data Management "**, look over here on the right side of the screen.
* And select **" Manage Software "** .
* Let's tap on it.
* Find and select the specific game title that is showing the corruption error.
* Scroll right down to the bottom over here.
* And select **" Check for Corrupt Data "** .
* Let's tap on it.
* Wait a moment while the system scans the game files for errors.
* If corrupted data is detected, select **" Redownload "** .
* Wait for the fresh game files to finish downloading from Nintendo's servers.
* Finally, launch the game and check if it opens without any corrupted data error.

And that's how you can fix a corrupted microSD card on your Nintendo Switch without losing your data.
