Found out your Nintendo Switch SD card is corrupted?
Don't panic! Before deleting anything, let's try a few ways to recover access to your data.
SOLUTION 1: REPAIR THE MICROSD CARD USING WINDOWS
•  First, turn off your Nintendo Switch completely.
•  Then, remove the microSD card from under the kickstand of your console.
•  Now, insert the microSD card into your computer using an SD card reader.
•  Next, press the " Windows Key " on your keyboard and type " cmd ".
•  Right-click on " Command Prompt " from the search results.
•  Select " Run as administrator ".
•  Click " Yes " if the User Account Control prompt appears.
•  Type " chkdsk E: /f /r " into the Command Prompt window.
•  Make sure to replace " E: " with the actual drive letter assigned to your microSD card in the windows File Explorer.
•  Press " Enter " on your keyboard.
•  Wait for Windows to scan the drive and repair any corrupted sectors.
•  Once the scan finishes, safely eject the microSD card from your computer.
•  Insert the microSD card back into your Nintendo Switch and power it on.
•  Finally, check if your games and data load without any error messages.
If Windows fails to repair the card or the drive is still unstable, let's move on to the next solution.
SOLUTION 2: MANUALLY BACK UP AND REFORMAT THE SD CARD
•  First, turn off your Nintendo Switch and take out the microSD card.
•  Connect the microSD card to your computer using a card reader.
•  Open " File Explorer " and click on your microSD card drive.
•  Right-click on the " Nintendo " folder and select " Copy ".
•  Paste the " Nintendo " folder onto your computer's desktop to create a safe backup.
•  Once the copy is finished, safely eject the microSD card from your PC.
•  Insert the microSD card back into your Nintendo Switch and turn it ON.
•  From the HOME Menu, open " System Settings ".
•  Scroll down the left sidebar and select " System ".
•  Scroll down on the right side and choose " Formatting Options ".
•  Select " Format microSD Card ".
•  Select " Continue " and confirm to format the card.
•  Wait for your Nintendo Switch to format the card and restart.
•  Turn off your console again and plug the microSD card back into your computer.
•  Copy your backed-up " Nintendo " folder from your desktop.
•  Paste it directly onto the root of your formatted microSD card.
•  Once the transfer is complete, safely eject the card from your PC.
•  Insert the microSD card back into your Nintendo Switch and turn it on.
•  Finally, check if your games and data load properly without any issues.
If only a specific game is still giving you a corruption error, proceed to the final solution.
SOLUTION 3: CHECK AND REDOWNLOAD CORRUPTED GAME DATA
•  First, from the HOME Menu open " System Settings ".
•  Scroll down on the left sidebar and select " Data Management ".
•  On the right side, select " Manage Software ".
•  Find and select the game title that is showing the corruption error.
•  Scroll down to the bottom and select " Check for Corrupt Data ".
•  Wait a moment while the system scans the game files for errors.
•  If corrupted data is detected, select " Redownload ".
•  Wait for the fresh game files to finish downloading from Nintendo's servers.
•  Finally, launch the game and check if it opens without any corrupted data error.
And that's how you can fix a corrupted microSD card on your Nintendo Switch without losing your data.
