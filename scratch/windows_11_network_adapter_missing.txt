Is your Windows 11 network adapter suddenly missing from Device Manager and you have no internet?
Don't worry—I've got a quick step-by-step fix that'll restore your adapter in just a couple of minutes.
So let's get started!

STEP 1: ADD LEGACY NETWORK ADAPTER DRIVER
* First, right-click on your **Start button**.
* From the menu, select **Device Manager**.
* Once inside, click on **Network adapters**.
* From the top menu bar, click on **Action**.
* Next, select **Add legacy hardware**.
* When the wizard appears, click on **Next**.
* Select **Install the hardware that I manually select from a list**.
* Now go ahead and click on **Next**.
* Looking down the list, select **Network adapters**.
* Click on **Next** to continue.
* From the left list, select your **Manufacturer**.
* On the right list, select your **Model**.
* Click on **Next** to begin installation.
* Click on **Next** once more to confirm.
* And finally, click on **Finish** to close.

Now, if adding the legacy driver didn't bring your network adapter back, your Windows network socket might be corrupted, so let's reset it using Command Prompt.

STEP 2: RESET WINSOCK AND TCP/IP STACK
* First, click on your **Start button**.
* Type " cmd " into the search box.
* Right-click right on **Command Prompt**.
* From the menu, select **Run as administrator**.
* When prompted, click on **Yes** to confirm.
* Type " netsh winsock reset " into the window.
* Press Enter on your keyboard to run it.
* Next, restart your PC to apply the reset.
* And finally, check if your network adapter appears.

Still not seeing your network adapter in the list? Let's perform a full network reset in Windows settings to reinstall all network components from scratch.

STEP 3: PERFORM A FULL NETWORK RESET
* First, press the windows key and " I " on your keyboard.
* Next, from the left menu, click on **Network & internet**.
* Scroll down and click on **Advanced network settings**.
* Under more settings, select **Network reset**.
* Now click right on the **Reset now** button.
* When the prompt appears, click on **Yes**.
* Let's give your PC a few moments to restart.
* And finally, check **Device Manager** for your adapter.

If none of these software solutions restored your network adapter, it's most likely an internal hardware failure with your Wi-Fi card or motherboard, so you'll want to contact support for a hardware repair.
Otherwise, your network adapter and internet connection should now be completely back to normal!
