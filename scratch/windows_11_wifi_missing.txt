Is your Windows 11 Wi-Fi suddenly gone and the option won't show up?
Don't worry—I've got a quick step-by-step fix that'll bring it right back in minutes.
So let's get started!

STEP 1: RESTART AND CONFIGURE WLAN AUTOCONFIG SERVICE
* First, click on your **Start button** on the taskbar.
* Next, type " services " into the search box.
* From the results, click right on the **Services** app.
* Once inside, scroll all the way down the list.
* Locate and right-click on **WLAN AutoConfig**.
* From the menu, select **Restart**.
* Let's give it a few moments to restart.
* Next, double-click on **WLAN AutoConfig**.
* Click on the **Startup type** drop-down menu.
* From the options, select **Automatic**.
* Go ahead and click on **Apply**.
* And then click on **OK** to save your changes.

Now, if your Wi-Fi icon still hasn't appeared, your wireless network adapter might simply be frozen, so let's quickly reset it.

STEP 2: RESTART YOUR WI-FI NETWORK ADAPTER
* First, press the windows key and " R " on your keyboard.
* In the Run box, type " ncpa.cpl " and press Enter.
* Once the connections window opens, locate your **Wi-Fi** adapter.
* Right-click directly on your **Wi-Fi** adapter.
* From the context menu, select **Disable**.
* Let's wait about five seconds.
* Next, right-click on your **Wi-Fi** adapter again.
* From here, go ahead and select **Enable**.
* And finally, check if your Wi-Fi option reappears.

If none of these software fixes brought your Wi-Fi back, it's most likely an issue with your internal Wi-Fi card, so you'll want to inspect your hardware.
Otherwise, your Wi-Fi option should now be completely restored and working normally!
